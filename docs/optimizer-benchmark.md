# QatlIA optimizer benchmark

Measured results for QatlIA's 2D guillotine cutting optimizer, produced by a
script in this repository from fixtures in this repository. Nothing below is
typed by hand: the tables are the verbatim output of the benchmark runner.

Re-running the command below on the same commit reproduces this file's tables
byte for byte — no run date is recorded here, because a hand-typed date is
itself an unverified claim and the tables carry no timestamp to check it
against.

## Reproducing this document

```bash
npm run benchmark:optimizer                       # stable JSON report
npm run benchmark:optimizer -- --format=markdown  # the tables below
```

The runner is `scripts/benchmark-optimizer.mjs`. It loads every fixture in
`tests/fixtures/benchmarks/`, calls the production `optimizeCutting2D` from
`src/lib/cutting/binpacking.ts`, re-verifies the returned geometry, and exits
non-zero if any fixture breaks an invariant or its published ceiling — so this
page cannot drift from the code without the command failing.

## Methodology

**Inputs.** Two fixtures, both measured under one identical configuration:
sheet 278 cm (height, Y) × 208 cm (width, X), kerf 0.3 cm, edge margin 1 cm on
all four sides, rotation allowed on every piece, grain not locked, material
separation off, objective `linear_guillotine`. Comparing plans produced at
different settings is meaningless, so the fixtures are not allowed to vary any
of it; `tests/benchmark-fixtures.test.js` fails if one does.

**What is measured.**

* *Source rows* — lines as written on the artisan's cut list.
* *Pieces* — source rows multiplied by their quantity, i.e. panels to cut.
* *Piece area* — the quantity-expanded piece area, in m², rounded to 4 decimals.
* *Sheets used / placed / unplaced* — read from the returned plan. Every
  expanded piece is asserted to appear exactly once as either placed or
  unplaced.
* *Utilization* — placed piece area divided by the **gross** area of every
  sheet the plan used. The 1 cm edge margin counts as sheet area, not as usable
  area, so this figure is the honest one an artisan pays for. Waste is
  100 − utilization. Both are rounded to 1 decimal.
* *Candidates evaluated* — how many candidate layouts the optimizer scored
  before selecting one.

**Area lower bound.** The runner also reports an *optimistic* floor on sheet
count: total piece area divided by the usable inner rectangle of one sheet
(the sheet minus the margin on all four sides), rounded up. It deliberately
excludes the kerf consumed by every saw pass, the guillotine (edge-to-edge)
cutting constraint, and all rectangle-packing loss — it treats the pieces as if
they could be poured into the sheet as a liquid. It is a floor, never a target,
and is never presented as "the optimum".

**Verification.** The runner independently re-checks each plan rather than
trusting the optimizer's own bookkeeping: pieces partition into placed/unplaced
exactly once, every placed piece lies inside its sheet, and no two placed
pieces on a sheet overlap. The same invariants are asserted by
`tests/optimizer-regression.test.js` on every test run.

**Rounding.** Areas 4 decimals, percentages 1 decimal, cut length 1 decimal.
Every figure is rounded exactly once, in the runner, at those precisions.

### Reading the results

* `standard-135` — 21 source rows, **135 pieces**, all placed, on **4 sheets** at **83.2 %** utilization. The area lower bound for this instance is also 4, so 4 is the minimum sheet count any optimizer could achieve here: the pieces do not fit into fewer sheets even ignoring kerf and packing loss entirely.
* `standard-16` — 5 source rows, **16 pieces**, all placed, on **2 sheets** at **75.3 %** utilization. The area lower bound for this instance is also 2, so 2 is the minimum sheet count any optimizer could achieve here: the pieces do not fit into fewer sheets even ignoring kerf and packing loss entirely.

Every measured instance above happens to meet its area lower bound. That proves minimal sheet count **for these instances only**, and says nothing about optimality in general — QatlIA does not claim global optimality for any other input.

## The planned 51-piece fixture is not published

The improvement plan listed a third quality gate for a 51-piece fixture. It
is **not published here, because its source data is unavailable**: no
dataset in this repository expands to 51 pieces. The nearest checked-in saved
run, `run_02_glass_bonding_6mm`, is a different job altogether — glass panels on
jumbo sheets, at different dimensions — so it cannot substitute for it, and no
piece will be invented or dropped to manufacture the count. The gate will be
added if and when the real source cut list is recovered.

## Comparison with other optimizers

QatlIA publishes no comparative result against CutOptim or any other tool. A
comparison is only meaningful when the other tool is run on this exact fixture,
at the sheet size, kerf, margin, rotation and material settings listed above,
and that has not been done. Any comparative claim will be added here only
together with the settings and the reproduction steps that produced it.

## Scope and honesty notes

* These are two real workshop cut lists, not a statistical sample. They support
  statements about *these instances*; they do not support a fleet-wide average
  waste-reduction or usable-area percentage, and no such percentage is
  advertised anywhere in the product.
* Utilization is reported against gross sheet area. Quoting it against the
  post-margin usable area would look better and mean less.
* The historical result summaries in `saved_runs/README.md` predate this
  methodology and are kept only as an archive; they are not evidence.
